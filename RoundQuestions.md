# Section D1 Workshop: The Data Detective Challenge

**Duration:** 120 minutes  
**Instructions:** Work in teams of 3-4. Use the actual charts, tables, and printed outputs from the previous sections. Support every claim with a visible feature of an output, a number from the dataset, or a clearly stated assumption. Record your answers before moving to the next round.

## Round 1 - Evidence Hunt (15 minutes)

1. From the histogram and summary statistics, identify one order or group of orders that could strongly influence the overall story. What would change if those observations were removed?
2. Choose one visual pattern that looks strong but may be weak numerically. Which statistic or additional chart would you use to check it?
3. For the `Discount_Percent` versus `Profit` plot, name one variable that could explain the scatter. Explain how that variable might create a misleading relationship.
4. Based only on the outputs so far, predict which variable will have the strongest relationship with `Profit`. Give a direction and a reason; do not calculate it yet.

## Round 2 - Competing Interpretations (20 minutes)

5. One analyst says, "Higher discounts destroy profit." Another says, "Unprofitable products simply receive higher discounts." Use the scatter plot and category outputs to argue both sides. What evidence would distinguish the explanations?
6. Pick a category or segment where the mean could hide an important customer story. Would you report the mean, median, range, or interquartile range first? Why?
7. The prosecution says the widest violin identifies the worst segment for planning. The defence says it only identifies the most diverse segment. Which argument is stronger, and what extra measure would settle the disagreement?
8. Select the darkest cell in the Segment x Category heatmap. Is it necessarily the best business opportunity? List two reasons why a high average profit cell might still be a poor choice for investment.

## Round 3 - Rebuild the Story (20 minutes)

9. Create a three-sentence executive story using one distribution plot, one comparison plot, and one relationship plot. The story must include one uncertainty or limitation.
10. Find two outputs that could appear to disagree, such as a category average and a segment distribution. Explain how both can be correct at the same time.
11. Replace one existing chart with a different chart type. State the question your replacement answers better and the question it answers worse.
12. Rewrite your main finding for (a) a warehouse manager, (b) a finance manager, and (c) a customer-retention manager. Keep the evidence the same but change the decision it supports.

## Round 4 - Business Decisions Under Uncertainty (20 minutes)

13. You have a fixed promotional budget. Would you invest in the category with the highest average profit, the segment-category pair with the highest heatmap value, or the category with the most orders? Choose one and defend the metric you rejected.
14. Propose a discount rule with three bands: safe, review-required, and prohibited. Use the observed relationship between discount and profit, and state what evidence is still missing before implementation.
15. Choose one segment, category, or payment mode for a returns-reduction pilot. Define the target group, the action, and the single outcome metric you would monitor.
16. List the three biggest risks in presenting the current visual findings to a senior stakeholder. For each, give one sentence that would make the presentation more honest.

## Round 5 - Mini Data Investigation (25 minutes)

17. Identify a possible outlier from the outputs. Decide whether it is a data error, a legitimate extreme order, or something you cannot determine. What check would you run?
18. If the next 100 orders were sampled differently from the current data, which conclusion would be most likely to change? Explain using sample size, spread, or imbalance.
19. Find the strongest positive or negative correlation in the heatmap. Explain why correlation does not prove that one variable causes the other. Design a small follow-up analysis that would improve the causal story.
20. A classmate gets a different chart from the same notebook. List four possible causes, ordered from most likely to least likely, and explain how to diagnose each.

## Round 6 - Prediction and Peer Review (20 minutes)

21. Write one null hypothesis and one alternative hypothesis for a claim made by your team. Predict whether the eventual p-value will be below or above 0.05, and explain why.
22. For your strongest finding, state the exact result or new observation that would make you withdraw or weaken the recommendation.
23. Exchange answers with another team. Mark each claim as descriptive, comparative, associational, or causal. Rewrite any causal claim that is not justified.
24. Prepare a 60-second briefing with exactly four parts: finding, evidence, business action, and limitation. End with one question that the statistical-testing section should answer.

## Final Reflection (5 minutes)

25. Which visual insight now feels least trustworthy, and why?
26. Which finding would you act on immediately despite uncertainty?
27. What is the most important question that the hypothesis tests in Section D2 must resolve?

**Suggested pacing:** Rounds 1-2 = 35 minutes, Rounds 3-4 = 40 minutes, Round 5 = 25 minutes, Round 6 = 15 minutes, Final Reflection = 5 minutes. Teams may present one answer from each round for a short whole-class discussion.
